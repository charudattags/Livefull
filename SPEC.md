# Livefull: Personalized Phase Plan Design

Oct 1, 2026 · @Safe Loup

## The goal: 276 days from stuck to self-respecting

The plan runs 276 days: a 21-day Foundation, a 75-day Challenge, then about 180 days of Becoming. That length is deliberate. Habits take a median of 59 to 66 days to form and can take up to 335 ([Singh et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/01/250124151347.htm)), so a shorter plan ends before most habits lock in.

"Stuck to self-respecting" is the internal north star, not the product's promise. Some of our users arrive through the "incel to gigachad" meme, and men who identify as incels report far higher depression, anxiety and loneliness than comparable men ([Costello et al.](https://liberalarts.utexas.edu/news/incels-are-not-particularly-right-wing-or-white-but-they-are-extremely-depressed-anxious-and-lonely-according-to-new-research)). "Gigachad" is a meme built on genetics and edited photos, and an unreachable target becomes one more reason to quit.

So the app promises only what the user controls:

- **Capable:** stronger, fitter, sleeping on a steady schedule.
- **Connected:** real conversations, a few close friends, a group you belong to.
- **Comfortable in your own skin:** groomed, dressed well, steadier self-talk.
- **Competent:** one skill or project you are visibly better at.

Progress is scored on behavior and capacity, never on looks ratings, matches or anyone else's reaction. Working tagline: *Become someone you respect.*

Gates check consistency at any tier, not perfection. Missing one means repeating a week or a block, never starting over.

Livefull is one app for all adults 18+. The social-confidence features work for everyone.

## Round 2 research: what changes the design

The biggest new finding is that reminders work against habits. App reminders raise repetition but leave weaker habits than cues tied to existing routines, so the app must fade its own notifications over time.

| Topic | What the evidence says | What we build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Habit timing | Median 59 to 66 days, range 4 to 335, and in one study only 23% reached the habit threshold at all. Morning and self-chosen habits came out stronger ([Singh 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)) | Show the real timeline on day 1; users pick their habits; prefer morning slots when they fit |
| Reminders vs cues | App reminders helped people remember but produced weaker habits than routine-event cues. Self-chosen clock times and routine cues did equally well in one planning trial (Keller 2021, in the Singh review) ([Stawarz 2015](https://medium.com/net-magazine/designing-health-apps-b0786409f256)) | Every habit anchors to an event or a time you pick; app notifications taper off by phase |
| Loneliness | 25% of US men aged 15 to 34 felt lonely a lot of the previous day, vs 18% of young women ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/690788/younger-men-among-loneliest-west.aspx)); other surveys find smaller gaps ([AIBM](https://aibm.org/research/male-loneliness-and-isolation-what-the-data-shows/)) | Social is a core pillar, not an add-on. No India figure found yet |
| The liking gap | After conversations, people underestimate how much the other person liked them, even months later ([Boothby 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30183512/)) | A predict-then-rate log after each social rep, so users see their own gap |
| Asking questions | People who ask more questions, especially follow-ups, are liked more, including in speed dating ([Huang 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28447835/)) | Conversation drills built on one follow-up question |
| What partners value | Across 45 countries (N = 14,399), both sexes ranked kindness and intelligence highest ([Walter 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/eprint/8ZXW9DCZXKPRXEUXRRVG/full)) | Dating readiness centers on character, conversation and competence, not looks hacks |
| Exercise and mood | Walking or jogging, yoga and strength training reduced depression, more so at higher intensity; strength and yoga had the lowest dropout ([Noetel 2024](https://portal.findresearcher.sdu.dk/en/publications/effect-of-exercise-for-depression-systematic-review-and-network-m)) | Training is framed as mood support; strength work is the default. It never replaces treatment |
| Sleep and testosterone | One week at 5 hours a night cut daytime testosterone 10 to 15% in 10 young men ([Leproult 2011](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/1029127)) | Sleep is the honest "natural T" lever; the app sells no boosters |
| Social media limits | Capping three apps at 10 minutes each for 3 weeks cut loneliness and depression in 143 students ([Hunt 2018](https://digitalwellbeing.org/reducing-social-media-to-30-mins-day-improves-wellbeing/)); results for quitting completely are mixed | Limit, don't ban |
| Self-compassion | Self-compassion after a failure raised motivation to improve, across four experiments ([Breines and Chen 2012](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-science-of-willpower/201206/does-self-compassion-or-criticism-motivate-self-improvement)) | Miss-day messages use self-compassion, never shame |
| Looksmaxxing forums | An analysis of 8,000+ comments found pushes toward surgery, bone-smashing and self-harm ([Halpin 2025](https://www.dal.ca/news/media/media-releases/2025/06/02/media_opportunity__increasingly_popular__looksmaxxing__sites_can_harm_rather_than_help_young_men__making_some_feel_like_failures_in_the__manosphere___dalhousie_university_research.html)) | No face ratings, no attractiveness scores, no surgery talk |
| Protein | Gains from extra protein plateau near 1.6 g per kg per day (49 studies, 1,863 people) ([Morton 2018](https://academicworks.cuny.edu/le_pubs/209/)) | A ceiling, shown only on opt-in; food first, supplements optional |
| AI coaching | A 12-month trial found full dynamic coaching no better than light coaching for activity ([Blondeel 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12700254)); a small 4-week LLM study reportedly improved enjoyment but not behavior (secondhand, verify) | The AI's job is to make the right action easy, not to talk more |

| "21 days to a habit" | Not supported; the median is about two months | Phase 1 installs habits; it doesn't claim to finish them |
| Mewing | No good evidence it reshapes an adult jaw (approximate, not searched) | Not offered as a face fix |
| Bone-smashing, black-market hormones, limb surgery | Harmful | Refused, with a plain explanation and a better alternative |

## The five rings

Five rings cover the whole person. Each user weights them differently, and Phase 1 starts with only two.

| Ring | Covers | Phase 1 starter (2-minute version) | Tracked by | Never |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Body | Training, steps, food basics, grooming, skin, hair, clothes, posture, voice | 5 push-ups or a 10-minute walk after an existing event; or wash face and lay out clothes the night before | Strength retests, steps, routine done; optional private photo log | Steroids, SARMs, crash diets, ranking bodies, face ratings, symmetry scores, surgery talk |
| Mind | Mood, focus, self-talk, stress, reflection, journal | One line at night: what went right today | WHO-5 wellbeing every 2 weeks, mood trend | Diagnosis or therapy claims |
| People | Conversations, friendships, belonging, dating readiness (the social ladder) | Greet one person you see daily and ask one question | Reps attempted, predict-vs-rate gap, weekly meaningful contacts | Pickup tactics, scoring people, counting matches |
| Craft | Study, career skills, money, one project | One 25-minute focus block on the most important task | Focus blocks, project milestones | All-nighter hustle culture |
| Rest | Sleep, phone, room, digital diet | Phone charges away from the bed; one app timer | Wake-time regularity, time on chosen apps, room reset done | Total phone bans |

The People ring measures what you do (attempts, follow-up questions, invitations), never how others respond. That keeps the score inside your control and keeps other people from becoming rewards.

For students, the Craft ring expands into a full study and extracurricular track: Study.

## The personalization engine

A plan is unique because it is built from the user's real week, not from randomness. Two users with the same goal get different plans because their Tuesdays, cues and failure patterns differ.

&#91;embedded content: personalization loop · 6 parts, weekly cycle\]

Daily logs feed a weekly review, which updates both the user model and the cards. The safety layer sits under everything.

### 1. Intake (days 0 to 7)

- **Day 0, about 10 minutes:** who you want to become, realistic minutes per day, fixed anchors (wake time, classes or work, meals, commute), equipment, budget, food pattern (veg, egg, non-veg), birth year (the age gate comes first), a short safety screen (exercise readiness, WHO-5 wellbeing), and a 1-to-5 tap on about 8 candidate cards for how much you want each.
- **Days 1 to 3, passive baseline:** sleep and wake times, steps and screen time from phone health and usage APIs, with consent.
- **Day 3 micro-tests:** max push-ups (knee version allowed), plank hold, a timed 1 km walk or jog, and "how many conversations over 10 minutes this week?"
- **Days 1 to 7:** seven onboarding modules, one short chat a day:
  1. **Identity and goals:** who you want to become, and the first proof you're them. Your answer is your first identity statement.
  2. **Motivation style:** gains vs avoiding losses; rules, reasons or freedom.
  3. **Accountability:** whether you keep promises to others better than to yourself, and whether you push back when told what to do.
  4. **Energy and context:** chronotype, commute, class or work hours, and which days are chaos.
  5. **Failure pattern:** what derailed you last time (perfectionism, boredom, burnout, social pressure), and what you did after a miss.
  6. **Game and tone:** points, quiet progress, a rolling consistency score, or none (never streaks); blunt, warm or funny.
  7. **Support and boundaries:** who you might share a weekly summary with yourself, and topics that are off-limits.

### 2. User model

Each answer becomes a hypothesis with a confidence score. Behavior updates it. If you say "morning person" but log nothing before 11 am, the model shifts and tells you why.

### 3. Plan compiler

- **Card library:** routine cards tagged with ring, item type, minutes, cost, equipment, evidence level, contraindications and three tiers (bad day, standard, hard).
- **Keystone pick:** score each candidate on impact, ease and want (1 to 5 each, multiplied). Impact comes from the card library, ease from minutes and equipment against your constraints, and want from your onboarding taps. Ties go to the card with fewer minutes. Take the top two, capped at 10 minutes a day in week 1, counting each card's 2-minute tier. This is our heuristic, not research.
- **Scheduler:** places every card after a real event in your week, or at a clock time you chose. The app's own notification is never the cue, since reminders weaken habits. You log with a tap in the app, and you confirm event anchors yourself; the app doesn't detect them. At most one check-in notification goes out per time window you chose, as a logging prompt, and these taper by phase.
- **Anti-plan:** your failure pattern sets the guardrails. Perfectionism gets bad-day tiers from day 1. Boredom gets rotating variants every 2 weeks. Burnout gets a cap on daily minutes.

### 4. Language layer (the LLM)

The model writes each if-then plan around your cues, in your words: "After I lock my cycle at the hostel gate, I do 10 push-ups before going up." Rules decide what and when; the LLM decides how it is said.

The coach's personality, style learning, voice and photo features are designed in Coach.

### 5. Weekly adaptation (our rules, to test)

- Completion above 90% and, where you tapped effort, effort rated easy: move that card up one tier.
- Completion below 60% for 2 weeks: shrink it to the 2-minute version or move its anchor.
- Three misses on the same weekday: that day gets the bad-day tier by default.
- In Phase 1, a new ring unlocks only when current keystones hold at 70% or better for 2 weeks. From Phase 2 you pick five of the six item types yourself.
- Effort is an optional tap (easy / ok / hard) in the log, and rules that use it fire only when you gave it. When rules collide, the priority is: safety, then shrink, then weekday default, then tier-up.

### 6. Personal experiments

From day 15, the app runs small two-week tests on you (the first runs days 15 to 28, into Phase 2): morning vs evening training, phone outside the room vs app timers, gym vs home workouts. You keep whatever wins for you. With 7 days per arm, the copy says "early lean", never "winner". Nobody else has your results, so this is the deepest source of uniqueness.

**Where uniqueness comes from:** your anchors and cues, your constraints, your failure pattern, your ring weights, your own words, and your experiment results.

## Phase 1: Foundation (days 1 to 21)

Phase 1 installs two keystones and a steady sleep window. It does not claim to form habits, and day 1 says so: "21 days gets it started. About two months makes it automatic."

| Week | Focus | Daily load | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1: Observe | Baseline and keystone A | About 10 min | Intake, micro-tests, keystone A at 2-minute size. Sleep is logged, not enforced |
| 2: Anchor | Keystone B and a WOOP plan | 15 to 20 min | Keystone B added. A WOOP plan for the obstacle you named. A fixed wake time, with a wind-down cue at night |
| 3: Stretch | Standard tier and a first experiment | 20 to 30 min | Keystones that are holding move to standard tier. First personal experiment starts. Day 21 retest and review |

**The daily loop**

- Morning: one tap, or nothing if sleep syncs automatically.
- Daytime: keystones fire off their anchor events. No clock-time pings.
- Night: a 30-second log and one line of reflection. The coach replies only when it has something worth saying.

Day 1 must end with one finished action, however small. Early competence predicted who kept going in the fitness-app study from round 1.

The sleep window starts from a fixed wake time, then pulls bedtime into a steady range. Round 1's UK Biobank finding favors regularity within about an hour over any particular clock time.

**Gate into Phase 2**

- Keystone A done on at least 14 of 21 days, at any tier.
- Day 21 retest completed.
- You choose to start the 75. It is opt-in, never automatic.

If the gate isn't met, you repeat week 3 with smaller cards: the phase is extended by 7 days and every later phase shifts by the same amount. The copy says "repeat", never "fail".

## Phase 2: The 75 (days 22 to 96)

The 75 keeps 75 Hard's daily structure and public commitment but drops the reset to day one. A missed day lowers your score. It never erases your progress.

### Daily items, each in three tiers

You pick five of these six. Any tier counts as done. Each item type belongs to one ring. Your cards (for example Train, Read, Portfolio site, Lights out) are the specific things you do; each card has one item type and one ring.

| Item | Bad day | Standard | Hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Move (Body) | 10-minute walk | Strength session 3 days a week, a 30-minute walk on the others | Strength 4 days a week plus a daily step goal |
| Fuel (Body) | Protein with one meal | Protein with every meal | Meals planned for the week |
| Sleep (Rest) | Wake time held | Wake time within 30 minutes, phone out of bed | Full window held all week |
| Social rep (People) | Greet someone and ask one question | One rep at your ladder level | Ladder rep plus one invitation a week |
| Craft (Craft) | One 25-minute focus block | Two blocks | Three blocks, with a weekly output shared |
| Reflect (Mind) | One line | 2-minute journal | Written weekly review |

### Rules

- **No reset.** Days count at any tier.
- **Never miss twice.** After a miss, the next day defaults to the bad-day tier. Lally found one missed day didn't hurt habit formation; long gaps did.
- **Rest counts.** One planned rest day a week for training items.
- **Finish line:** at least 60 of 75 days logged at any tier.

### Five 15-day blocks

| Block | Plan days | Theme | What's added |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Build | 22 to 36 | Strength base | Full-body training 3 days a week; social ladder steps 1 and 2 |
| 2. Expand | 37 to 51 | Make training automatic | Grooming routine, clothes that fit; ladder step 3 |
| 3. The dip | 52 to 66 | Expect the slump | A fresh-start landmark on day 52 (not a reset); difficulty held flat; a weekly summary you can share with someone you choose |
| 4. Depth | 67 to 81 | Skill | A craft project with a visible output; ladder step 4 |
| 5. Proof | 82 to 96 | Retest and show | Full retest; one result you choose to share; ladder step 5 |

Phase 1 keystones cross the 59 to 66 day median during blocks 3 and 4. So 66 days after each keystone starts, it gets a 4-item automaticity check (the SRBAI, used in the habit review).

### The social ladder

1. Greet someone you see daily and ask one question.
2. Ask a follow-up question, then listen to the answer.
3. Hold a 5-minute conversation with a classmate, colleague or stranger.
4. Invite someone to something low-stakes: chai, a game, a study session.
5. Join or host a small group activity.
6. Optional, only if dating is your goal: ask someone out once, clearly and respectfully, and accept a no gracefully.

Default pace: steps 1 and 2 in block 1, step 3 in block 2, step 4 in block 4 and step 5 in block 5. Step 6 is optional and never scheduled by default.

Before each rep you predict how it will go, 1 to 10. Afterwards you rate how it went. The app charts your personal liking gap, which is usually more convincing than any pep talk.

### Body, realistically

Training follows a beginner full-body program with progressive overload: add a rep or a little weight once every set is clean. For fat loss, targets are capped near 0.5 to 1% of body weight a week (a common guideline, approximate). Anyone with eating-disorder flags never gets weight or calorie targets. Flags come only from intake answers or phrases the keyword matcher catches, never from inference.

### Gate into Phase 3

- At least 60 of 75 days logged at any tier.
- Retest and WHO-5 completed.
- You rewrite your identity statement for the next six months (the first one came from onboarding module 1).

Below 60 days, the screen offers two choices: "repeat block 5" (the phase is extended by 15 days and later phases shift) or "lighter Phase 3".

## Phase 3: Becoming (days 97 to 276)

Phase 3 hands control from the app to you. Prompts taper, plans become co-written, and you write the last cycle alone. Habits tied to app reminders tend to fade when the app goes quiet, so independence is the goal, not a side effect.

### Three 8-week cycles and a graduation

| Stage | Plan days | Theme | Who plans | Prompts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cycle 1 | 97 to 152 | Strength and standing | App drafts, you edit | Event cues plus a few nudges |
| Cycle 2 | 153 to 208 | Connection and contribution | You draft, app reviews | Event cues; a nudge only after 2 misses |
| Cycle 3 | 209 to 264 | Ownership | You plan alone | None by default; weekly review only |
| Graduation | 265 to 276 | Maintenance | You write your protocol | Monthly check-in afterwards |

Every cycle opens on a fresh-start landmark (a new month or semester), runs one personal experiment, and ends with a deload week and a retest.

### What each cycle adds

- **Cycle 1, strength and standing:** training moves past beginner basics, the presence routine locks in, and you pick one craft project with a finish line inside Phase 3.
- **Cycle 2, connection and contribution:** you join or build a recurring group, such as a sports team, club, study circle or volunteer shift. You also help someone a step behind you. Status research separates prestige, earned through skill and generosity, from dominance, earned through fear ([Royal Society 2025](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2025.0149)). The app only coaches the first.
- **Cycle 3, ownership:** you choose which rings get a stretch goal and set the tiers yourself. The app reviews but doesn't direct.

### Graduation (days 265 to 276)

- Full retest against the day 3 baseline, plus your WHO-5 trend.
- A **maintenance protocol** you write: the minimum version of each habit you keep for life.
- A **relapse plan**, for example: "If I miss two weeks, I restart with bad-day tiers for 7 days."
- The app drops to a monthly check-in.

### What realistically changes by day 276

- **Body:** clearly stronger than your baseline, and training feels normal, not heroic.
- **Mind:** exercise gives a real mood lift, though it is not a cure.
- **People:** a regular group and a few people you talk to every week.
- **Body (presence):** grooming and clothes you no longer have to think about.
- **Craft:** one finished project you can show someone.

Some habits will still be under construction, since the habit review's range runs to 335 days. The app says that plainly at graduation.

## Guardrails

The app refuses a short list of things outright, routes risk to humans, and blocks under-18 users. These rules sit above the LLM and above any user preference.

### Never

- Rate faces or bodies, or show attractiveness or "market value" scores.
- Suggest steroids, SARMs, black-market hormones, bone-smashing, limb surgery or crash diets.
- Treat women, or anyone, as a reward, target or score. No pickup scripts, no alpha/beta language.
- Use shame, streak-loss threats or guilt after a miss.
- Claim to diagnose or treat. It is a coach, not therapy, which is the Woebot lesson from round 1.
- Pretend to be human when someone asks.

### Safety routing

- **Low wellbeing:** WHO-5 is retaken every 2 weeks. A low score triggers a gentle suggestion to talk to a professional, with concrete options. The cutoff is a TODO constant until it is checked against the official WHO-5 scoring sheet.
- **Self-harm or suicide mentions:** coaching stops. The reply is warm and direct and shows crisis options. In India that is Tele-MANAS, free and 24/7 in 20 languages, at 14416 or 1-800-891-4416 ([Tele-MANAS](https://citizenmatters.in/explained-how-tele-manas-is-shaping-tele-mental-healthcare-in-india/)), and 112 for emergencies. A keyword matcher (English, Hinglish, romanized Hindi) runs on the device and again in the Edge Function before any model call, and shows the inline crisis card with fixed text only. If you tap "I'm safe, keep talking", the coach sends a fixed supportive message and stays in support-only mode for the rest of the session: it listens and encourages contact with a person or helpline, with no plan or coaching talk. The card stays pinned, and any new crisis phrase shows it again. The model's `CRISIS` sentinel is a backstop after the call. A clinician reviews the phrase list before launch.
- **Disordered-eating signs** (skipping meals to cut, exercising to cancel food): weight and calorie numbers switch off everywhere, and the app suggests support. Flags come only from intake answers or keyword-matcher phrases, never from inference.
- **Exercise readiness:** chest pain, fainting or a known heart condition at intake means a doctor's clearance before training. Until you confirm clearance, the plan is walking-only.

### Age: 18+ only

The age gate asks your birth year and blocks anyone under 18. India's DPDP Act bars behavioural monitoring of under-18s even with parental consent, so there is no teen mode in v1. It comes back only if a lawyer clears it (see the Build plan tab).

## Build notes

Rules handle timing, scoring and safety; the LLM handles language and judgment. That split keeps the app cheap, testable and predictable.

### Agent layers

1. **Event layer (rules, on-device where possible):** records the check-ins you tap, schedules at most one logging prompt per time window you chose, and computes rolling scores. You confirm event anchors; the app doesn't detect them. On Android, an optional first-unlock or place cue can only open a prompt window; completion is always your tap.
2. **Safety layer:** screens every user message before the coach sees it. A keyword matcher runs on the device and in the Edge Function before any model call. Crisis paths are fixed flows, never generated text.
3. **Daily coach (small LLM):** writes at most 2 coach-started messages a day from a short state summary, and replies to up to 20 of your messages a day, within a daily token budget. These are config constants, tuned from real cost data.
4. **Weekly planner (larger LLM plus rules):** reviews the week, applies the adaptation rules, and proposes changes the user approves.
5. **User model:** the hypothesis profile with confidence scores, visible and editable by the user.

### Platforms, language and plan day

- **Android** is the full app: encrypted local database, offline logging, journal vault, Health Connect, screen-capture blocking and geofences.
- **Web** is a full client but online-only in v1. It has no local database, so nothing unencrypted sits in the browser. The journal vault, Health Connect, screen-capture blocking and geofences are Android-only, and web shows "available on Android".
- **Language:** the UI is English, with every string in one file. Hinglish is coach-only in v1.
- **Share my weekly summary:** the summary is built on the device and opens the Android share sheet (the Web Share API on web, falling back to copy), so you send it yourself. The app sends it to no one.
- **Plan day** is calendar days since start. Phase start dates are stored, not fixed numbers. A failed gate extends that phase (week 3 by 7 days, block 5 by 15 days) and every later phase shifts. 276 days is the length with no repeats.
- **A day ends at 4 am local** by default, configurable in Me. Logs carry the local date from the profile timezone.

| Sleep regularity | Spread of wake times over 14 days | User |
| Automaticity | SRBAI per keystone, 66, 152 and 276 days after that keystone starts (the last check no later than graduation) | User |
| Independence | Share of completions on days with no prompt sent | Team north star |
| Retention | Day 1, 7, 30 and 90 retention, plus phase-gate pass rates | Team |

Team metrics are aggregate counts computed on the server. There is no third-party analytics SDK in v1.

Independence is the metric that keeps the team honest. If it isn't rising through Phase 3, the app is building dependence on itself, not habits.
